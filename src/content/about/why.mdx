## Why CODs exist

In emergencies, many organizations provide relief, each in its own way, and each collects
information as the response goes on: needs assessments, where partners are working, how many
people have been reached. Sharing this information is how needs get identified and how gaps
and duplication in assistance are avoided. It only works if every organization refers to
places in the same way.

CODs give partners that common reference. When UN agencies, NGOs, the Red Cross and Red
Crescent, and governments use the same boundaries and population figures, their data can be
compared and combined. In each country, the Information Management Working Group, made up of
information managers from government, UN agencies, clusters and NGOs, adopts the CODs as the
standard.

## Preparedness

Decisions in the first hours and days of an emergency rely on data that already exists, so
the work of gathering, managing and sharing it has to happen before a crisis. For
sudden-onset emergencies, current guidance calls for CODs to be shared within 48 hours. That
leaves time to check and correct them, but not to build them. If CODs have been kept up to
date, the corrections are small.

## P-codes

Linking datasets by place name causes errors. Sources spell names differently, or use another
language: the Madagascar COD-AB has Amoron I Mania and Haute Matsiatra, where OpenStreetMap
has Amoron’i Mania and Matsiatra Ambony. Names also repeat. In Madagascar, 81 different
administrative level 4 units are called Morafeno.

<Morafeno />

A P-code (place code) identifies one administrative unit. It starts with the country’s ISO3
code and adds a three-digit number for each level below, separated by dots. Units that
share a name, like the commune of Morafeno and the fokontany inside it, still get different
codes.

<PcodeLevels />

Datasets that use the same P-codes can be joined directly, without matching names by hand.
Each COD-AB includes a gazetteer listing every P-code with its name. The <a href={`${standardsBase}specification/pcode/`}>P-code format</a> in the Standards gives the full rules.
